I didn’t choose most of my music.

97,427 plays·seven years·81% machine-queued·19% deliberate taps

a streamed “play” is produced jointly by a listener and a recommender, yet listening logs get read as records of taste. spotify’s export records who initiated every play (reason_start: did i tap this song, or did it just come on next). condition on that one field and most of what my own log seemed to say about me changes. streaming logs are not preference logs.

◢◤ the contamination argument

only 19% of my plays started with a deliberate act (pooled over a changing logger; the strict definition is 11.5% and era-stable). the other 81% were the platform advancing a queue. some summaries survive conditioning on who pressed play — my circadian structure does. genre composition doesn’t: the calm ambient wall that reads as “my taste” is mostly what the algorithm over-serves, and the ranking flips once exposure is separated from choice.

the sharpest result is a catch, and it travels: an embedding trained on an algorithm-dominated stream leaks its provenance into any statistic computed on its partitions. in normal words — an embedding is a learned map of which artists sit near each other. mine was learned mostly from plays the algorithm picked, so the map already drew its borders the algorithm’s way, and anything measured on that map made the algorithm look tidier than it is. my own headline number arrived inflated at 1.61. the audit caught it — and retraining at seven algorithmic-share levels traces a monotone dose-response between how contaminated the training stream is and how big the inflation gets. any pipeline that fits “preference” models to raw streaming logs inherits this.

◢◤ the finding, with its whole history

when i pick the next track, it crosses into another listening community (a cluster of artists that belong together; here, a genre family from public tags) more often than when autoplay picks. the bridge index is that ratio, with same-artist repeats removed from both channels: 1.05 (95% CI 1.03–1.08) on communities built only from external genre tags — no embedding anywhere — and 1.10 (1.08–1.13) on the higher-coverage tag partition where 39% of labels were filled in through my own listening embedding. same leakage channel, smaller dose, so i lead with the smaller number. every provenance-clean estimate lands between 1.03 and 1.14 — a partition-sensitivity range, not an interval estimate; fold shuffle into the algorithm’s side and the floor is 1.03, which doesn’t survive multiplicity correction (the penalty for testing many things: try enough cuts and one will look real by luck). the 1.15–1.30 band i reported earlier is withdrawn. here is every number we ever quoted for it, on one axis, including the dead ones:

how to read 1.05 (1.03–1.08) — my picks cross about 5% more often than autoplay’s: roughly 70 of every 100 jumps against 66. the bracket is the range the true value plausibly sits in, given how much data there is. it stays above 1 on the transitions i can score, so the direction is clear. the size is not: a third to two fifths of my transitions have no public genre tag on this partition, and depending on how i treat that untagged tail the index lands anywhere from 1.00 to 1.13. so i report the direction and not the size. a partition is just one way of sorting artists into communities; i tried eight.

killed and demoted estimates struck; mechanical floors bracketed. single source: bi_single_source.json in the repro kit. pan sideways on phones.

whether the gap is listener agency or recommender design, one log can’t adjudicate. the operative fact is narrower: the two channels are structurally different, so a log that pools them misrepresents both.

◢◤ the audit is the product

every claim ran a fixed, pre-declared audit with kill conditions written before the result was seen. most claims died. 28 methods ran; 16 of my own findings are dead on the page, killed by the harness built to check them. a sample:

  • the predictability gapan in-sample mirage: d=0.50 collapses to −0.02 out-of-sample.
  • the 4-year reign “p = 0.0000”a straw-man null; a circular block bootstrap gives p = 0.49–0.78.
  • music → heart ratefails a shuffle-instrument test of exogeneity. null, twice.
  • “i’m choosing more over time”a logging artifact — the instrument changed, i didn’t.
  • genre trends over timemostly what was served, wearing the costume of what was wanted.
  • the portability democonfidently wrong about 696 strangers — the proxy doesn’t transfer.

all sixteen, with receipts: the graveyard.
