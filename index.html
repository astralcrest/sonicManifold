01
the contamination argument

streaming logs are not preference logs.

only 19% of my plays started with a deliberate act (pooled over a changing logger; the strict definition is 11.5% and era-stable). the other 81% were the platform advancing a queue.

some summaries survive conditioning on who pressed play — my circadian structure does. genre composition doesn’t: the calm ambient wall that reads as “my taste” is mostly what the algorithm over-serves, and the ranking flips once exposure is separated from choice.

the sharpest result is a catch, and it travels: an embedding trained on an algorithm-dominated stream leaks its provenance into any statistic computed on its partitions. in normal words — an embedding is a learned map of which artists sit near each other. mine was learned mostly from plays the algorithm picked, so the map already drew its borders the algorithm’s way, and anything measured on that map made the algorithm look tidier than it is. my own headline number arrived inflated at 1.61. the audit caught it — and retraining at seven algorithmic-share levels traces a monotone dose-response between how contaminated the training stream is and how big the inflation gets. any pipeline that fits “preference” models to raw streaming logs inherits this.

02
the transfer

the part that isn’t about music

i trained an artist embedding on this stream, which is 6.6× algorithm-dominated, and measured my own headline on it. it came back inflated at 1.61: the training stream’s provenance had leaked into the geometry. withdrawn

0.99→1.46dose-response across seven retrained embeddings, by algorithmic share

retraining at seven algorithmic-share levels traces that dose-response, 0.99 to 1.46. on a simulated stream where both channels cross communities at exactly the same rate, the same pipeline still read 1.11 at an 80% algorithmic share, and the curve stays where it is when i hold the number of communities fixed. any preference model fit to logs that a deployed system helped generate inherits this, music or not.

a recommender writes most of the log. a model trained on that log learns the recommender as well as the listener. most logs never say which was which. spotify’s export does, in one field, and this whole project is what happens when you read it. that is a measurement problem before it is a music problem.

the bridge index: how much more often my own pick crosses into another listening community than autoplay’s does. a direction, not a size.

when i pick the next track, it crosses into another listening community (a cluster of artists that belong together; here, a genre family from public tags) more often than when autoplay picks. the bridge index is that ratio, with same-artist repeats removed from both channels: 1.05 (95% CI 1.03–1.08) on communities built only from external genre tags — no embedding anywhere — and 1.10 (1.08–1.13) on the higher-coverage tag partition where 39% of labels were filled in through my own listening embedding. same leakage channel, smaller dose, so i lead with the smaller number. every provenance-clean estimate lands between 1.03 and 1.14 — a partition-sensitivity range, not an interval estimate; fold shuffle into the algorithm’s side and it reads 1.03, which doesn’t survive multiplicity correction (the penalty for testing many things: try enough cuts and one will look real by luck) — that is the floor of the comparator axis, not the floor of the study. the 1.15–1.30 band i reported earlier is withdrawn.

three numbers i published and then took down or trimmed, in the order it happened. the dashed blue branches are readings that drift to parity; the three ways that happens are below. pan sideways on phones.

three ways it goes to parity

  • the untagged taildepending on how i treat the transitions with no public genre tag, the index lands anywhere from 1.00 to 1.13.
  • the loosest definitionscount skip-presses as my taps, count shuffle as the algorithm’s, and it reads 1.01 (0.99–1.03), which no longer separates from 1.
  • matched tag coveragerestrict both sides to artists above 50 plays, where the gap in tag coverage closes to a fifth of a point, and it reads 1.01 (0.98–1.04). no higher cut-off gives a bracket that clears 1 either.
read the working: how to read 1.05, and what it held up to

how to read 1.05 (1.03–1.08) — my picks cross about 5% more often than autoplay’s: roughly 70 of every 100 jumps against 66. the bracket is the range the true value plausibly sits in, given how much data there is. it stays above 1 on the transitions i can score, so the direction is clear. the size is not: a third to two fifths of my transitions have no public genre tag on this partition, and depending on how i treat that untagged tail the index lands anywhere from 1.00 to 1.13. so i report the direction and not the size. there is a second way it goes to parity: take the loosest reading of both definitions — count skip-presses as my taps, count shuffle as the algorithm’s — and it reads 1.01 (0.99–1.03), which no longer separates from 1. there is a third: match the two channels on how well they are tagged. restrict both sides to artists above 50 plays, where the gap in tag coverage between them closes to a fifth of a point, and it reads 1.01 (0.98–1.04), and no higher cut-off gives a bracket that clears 1 either.

survived what it did hold up to: clustering the error bars on artist instead of session, splitting by device, splitting by logging era, and a label-permutation test that reshuffles who pressed play inside each session and never once reaches the observed value in 20,000 draws. a partition is just one way of sorting artists into communities; i tried eight.

whether the gap is listener agency or recommender design, one log can’t adjudicate. the operative fact is narrower: the two channels are structurally different, so a log that pools them misrepresents both.

every kill condition was written into code before i saw the result.

every claim ran a fixed, pre-declared audit, with the kill condition written in code before the result was seen. then i put the dashboard’s own back catalogue through the same gates: 11 of 25 headline claims survived. 16 of my own findings are dead on this page, each with a card naming the check that killed it. a sample:

  • the predictability gapan in-sample mirage: d=0.50 collapses to −0.02 out-of-sample.
  • the 4-year reign “p = 0.0000”a straw-man null; a circular block bootstrap gives p = 0.49–0.78.
  • music → heart ratefails a shuffle-instrument test of exogeneity. null, twice.
  • “i’m choosing more over time”a logging artifact — the instrument changed, i didn’t.
  • genre trends over timemostly what was served, wearing the costume of what was wanted.
  • the portability democonfidently wrong about 696 strangers — the proxy doesn’t transfer.
