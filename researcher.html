Streaming Logs Are Not Preference Logs: A Single-Subject Provenance Audit of the Human–Recommender Loop

astralcrest · Independent Researcher · astralcrestlab@proton.me

◢◤ Abstract

A logged “play” is jointly produced by listener and recommender, and Spotify’s export records each one’s provenance (reason_start). We audit a seven-year, 97,427-play single-subject log by conditioning every summary on it. Circadian structure and artist rankings survive. Genre composition does not, nor do a predictability gap and a music–physiology coupling; an exposure-bias reversal on its over-served pole does.

One failure is in the instrument, not the subject. Retraining the artist embedding across seven algorithmic-share levels at fixed transition count moves the same statistic from 0.99 to 1.46; balancing the corpus does not remove that bias, and the remedy is a partition the stream never trained. Our headline, deliberate transitions crossing communities more often than algorithmic ones, arrived inflated at 1.61. On external-tag partitions, with same-artist continuations dropped from both arms, the Bridge Index is 1.05 (session-block 95% CI [1.03, 1.08]); eight label-independent partitions span 1.03–1.14. The direction holds under partition, seed, resolution, era and device, and fails under the untagged tail (34–41% of transitions, imputations bracketing 1), under the most inclusive arm definitions, and under coverage matching, 1.008 [0.977, 1.038] once both arms clear 50 plays. Kill conditions were fixed in code, not registered.

Note on the abstract, which mirrors the paper’s word for word. The higher-coverage variant quoted throughout the page below is 1.10 [1.08, 1.13], and its nominal 95% interval is simulated to cover about 86% at an effect of that size. That flag travels with the number wherever it is quoted.

◢◤ Positioning vs. related work

The algorithmic-confounding lineage (Chaney et al.) establishes that user-behavior claims are invalid without accounting for exposure, but demonstrates it on simulated or rating-matrix data with an inferred or latent exposure variable; propensity-scoring methods likewise estimate a click propensity from the interaction matrix itself. This setting differs in what is observed: Spotify’s reason_start field records the initiation channel of each play directly. That is not the exposure variable the debiasing lineage estimates: their propensities concern which items were surfaced, including unshown items no listening log can see; this paper does not recover that counterfactual. What direct observation buys is narrower: for summaries of played items, deconfounding reduces to conditioning on an observed intent label rather than positing a latent exposure. Villermet et al. disentangle organic, algorithmic, and editorial curation across roughly nine thousand Deezer users and find the balance strongly user-dependent (“filter niches,” not a uniform bubble); Shakespeare et al. show that whether algorithmic curation reads as diversity-reducing depends on the scale of aggregation, so genre-level conclusions are inherently fragile to representation choices. Quantitatively their platform sits far to the organic side of this log: ~80% of Deezer plays are organically accessed platform-wide, the largest user class (62% of users) averages 94% organic access, and even the most algorithm-leaning class (11% of users) averages 58% algorithmic, against which this listener’s 81% machine-queued share sits deep in the algorithmic tail of that population. This paper is the single-subject, epistemic-audit counterpart to that population work: rather than characterize the curation mix, it asks which downstream summaries survive conditioning on a directly-observed intent signal, and the genre-level fragility result reproduces the scale- and heterogeneity-driven findings of that literature at n=1.

◢◤ Method in one screen

Provenance classes

Every play is stratified on reason_start into three tiers: direct tap (19.1%: clickrow/playbtn/backbtn/fwdbtn/remote; a strict deliberate-selection subset, clickrow/playbtn/remote, is 11.5%), autoadvance (80.2%: trackdone, the platform selecting the next queue item), and ambiguous (0.5%: appload/null/unknown, excluded from intent-stratified analyses). Independently, 16.7% of plays carry shuffle=True. Autoadvance and shuffle-driven plays together put the algorithmic-exposure share at roughly 95% of the corpus. Note: bundled 19.1% is not era-stable across the 2023-10 logger changepoint (11.8% before vs 22.7% after, 10.9pp swing); strict 11.5% swings 1.87pp under the pre-declared 3pp stability bar, though drifting slightly upward.

The gate sequence

Every candidate claim runs an ordered sequence of pass/fail gates, kill condition fixed in code before the result is seen:

0pre-declared kill condition → 0bmeasured-target precheck → 1chronological held-out eval → 2perturbation robustness → 3power analysis (nulls) → 4surrogate / permutation null → 5E-value confounder bound

killed at the first failed gate · caveated if the core gates pass but a bounding gate flags a limit · survived only if every applicable gate passes. Skipped gates are not passes.

The Bridge Index and the leakage correction

What the Index is. Splitting the log by provenance yields two transition graphs over the same artists: the listener’s deliberate taps and the platform’s autoadvance plays. Which artists depends on the partition, and the two populations differ enough to state separately. The embedding- and graph-based partitions run on the 804 artists with 20 or more plays. The external-tag partition that carries the headline is a different and larger set: 1,196 artists labelled into 13 families, of which 526 (44.0%) fall below that 20-play line, with a median of 23 qualified plays, and only 670 of the 804 appear in it at all. On communities detected from an intent-independent artist-embedding k-NN graph (Louvain, k=10, resolution 1.0), and restricted to different-artist transitions, with same-artist repeats removed from both arms, since the algorithm produces them more often (24.1% vs. the listener’s 18.9%) and they are trivially within-community on any partition: deliberate transitions cross community boundaries 58.8% of the time against autoadvance’s 51.0% (same-artist-controlled, balanced-partition rates, mid-session transitions exclusively; the crossing pool is mid-session by construction, as transitions with gaps >30 minutes never enter it). The Bridge Index is the ratio of the listener’s cross-community rate to the algorithm’s, always same-artist-controlled and always the rate over its own partition’s geometry-free floor. A separate floor comparison would restate the same ratio twice, so none is quoted.

Partition-selection rule. The Index is reported primarily on the leakage-free partition (built only from external Last.fm tags, with no label propagated through the stream-trained embedding) at the 13-genre-family granularity, which was fixed in the pre-declaration of the same-artist control before any controlled value was computed: 1.05 [1.03, 1.08]. Where the higher-coverage variant is quoted (the same 13 families including the 39% of artist labels propagated through the embedding’s k-NN; 45,210 controlled transitions against 31,814), it is labelled as such; at every matched granularity the propagated-label partition sits 0.03–0.05 above its leakage-free counterpart, the same leakage channel at a smaller dose. The balanced-embedding rates above are therefore a leakage-reduced upper bound, not the headline.

Session openers and crossing floors. Session-opening taps, built separately, show a controlled BI of 1.220 [1.157, 1.290], comparable to the mid-session headline, so deliberate steering is not concentrated only at session openers. Both streams cross far below the ~86–91% a blind random pick from the listener’s own catalogue would give (post-hoc diagnostic), so the gap is not free-crossing noise.

Menu authorship stratifies it. Menu authorship was re-estimated on the headline partition. With context_uri absent, a tap on an algorithm-authored playlist is “deliberate” by taxonomy, so taps were split by how the destination artist first entered the library. Holding the denominator at the full autoplay arm gives 0.97 [0.93, 1.01] on self-introduced destinations (n=1,753), 1.07 [1.03, 1.11] on algorithm-introduced and 1.18 [1.13, 1.22] on shuffle-introduced. That ordering is a composition effect: the autoplay arm’s own crossing rate varies by the same destination class (0.633, 0.655, 0.712), and its destination mix differs from the tap arm’s. Splitting both arms on the same variable gives 1.016 [0.971, 1.065], 1.084 [1.046, 1.122] and 1.097 [1.053, 1.141]. The advantage is present on every destination class and is weakest on destinations the listener introduced, where it is not distinguishable from parity. Discovery provenance is a stratifier of the Index. It is not a confound the Index is robust to, and it is not evidence that the Index is carried by either region of the catalogue. An earlier version of this passage reported the opposite ordering from the balanced-embedding arm, which is withdrawn.

Coverage is the binding limit. On the headline partition 33.8% of deliberate and 40.9% of autoplay different-artist transitions are unscored because an endpoint carries no external tag. Assigning every untagged artist to one shared community gives 1.135 [1.109, 1.161]; assigning each untagged artist its own community gives 0.999 [0.983, 1.015]. The two imputations bracket 1, so the direction is reported and the magnitude is not. On the scored transitions the headline survives a Bonferroni correction over the eight partitions (p=0.0012), is stable across the 2023-09 era break (1.061 / 1.053), and exceeds a pooled intent-blind first-order surrogate (paired gap +0.056 [0.036, 0.078]).

Label accuracy, not only label coverage. Every crossing event compares two scraped Last.fm top tags mapped through a hand-curated 13-family taxonomy, and this study audits the coverage of those labels at length and their accuracy nowhere. A pre-declared run, gated on an exact four-decimal reproduction of the headline row, reassigns each of the 1,196 labelled artists to a different family with probability r, drawing one map per replicate and scoring both arms on that same map, so the injected error is non-differential by construction. Over 500 replicates per cell the median point estimate falls smoothly toward 1 without reaching it: 1.045, 1.040, 1.029 and 1.021 at r = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 and 0.30 with the replacement family drawn uniformly, and 1.045, 1.037, 1.025 and 1.015 with it drawn in proportion to family size (ledger 245). That is the conservative case by construction: non-differential error attenuates a ratio of rates toward 1 rather than through it, so the pre-declared kill condition, a median point estimate at or below 1, was close to unreachable. Only the point estimate was varied. Re-running the session-block bootstrap on noisy maps instead, over 50 maps per cell, the lower bound still clears 1 on 48 of 50 maps at a size-proportional r = 0.10, but on 33 of 50 at 0.20 and 20 of 50 at 0.35, so the point estimate is the durable part of this check and the exclusion of 1 is not. The claim is only that the point estimate is not brittle to modest non-differential tag error; nothing here measures the true rate at which these labels are wrong.

Differential label error, tested separately. Error concentrated in the sparse tail where tags are worst is the case that can move the estimate the other way, and a second pre-declared run tests it directly on the same reproduction gate (ledger 247). It splits the 1,196 labelled artists at the same 20-play line, 526 in the tail and 670 in the head, and reassigns the two strata at different rates on a pre-declared eight-cell grid rising to 0.30 in one stratum while the other is held at 0 or 0.05, both arms again scored on one shared map, 500 replicates per cell and scheme. Two results bound it. Among scored transitions the arms’ exposure to the thin-tag tail runs against the premise of the threat: an endpoint falls below the 20-play line on 13.9% of deliberate and 15.6% of autoadvance transitions, and for the destination alone the figures are 6.3% and 9.6%, so here it is autoplay rather than deliberate choice that reaches further into the tail. And on the real map the median point estimate attenuates toward 1 in every cell and in both directions of the asymmetry, from 1.048 when only the tail is noisy at rtail = 0.10 to 1.034 at (0.05, 0.30) and 1.024 at (0.30, 0.05) when the head is the noisy stratum, although individual replicates in the head-noisy cells do fall below 1 (a 2.5th percentile of 0.996). A companion planted null, in which family labels are permuted within the two popularity strata so that the true Index sits at 1, returned no cell median above 1.001 and no replicate at or above 1.05 in any of the sixteen cell-by-scheme combinations. That null is recorded as weak evidence, and why: a permuted map is already at the fixed point of the reassignment operator then applied to it, so the operator has almost nothing left to move, and the pre-declared threshold of a median at 1.02 was close to unfireable for that reason rather than because the world is kind. What the pair of runs supports is narrow. The point estimate attenuates in both directions instead of reversing. The session-block interval was not recomputed on differentially noisy maps, so nothing here says the exclusion of 1 would stand under that noise. The stratifier is the subject’s own play count rather than external obscurity, which is the variable tag quality actually tracks. And no number in either run measures the true differential error rate: settling it needs a hand audit of artists sampled in proportion to their transition mass, with the error rate reported against play count.

The leakage correction. The naive full-stream embedding gives 1.61 (same-artist transitions included): inflated because the embedding is trained on a stream that is 6.6× algorithm-dominated, so algorithm-adjacency shapes the communities themselves and deflates the algorithm’s measured cross-rate; same-artist continuation, which the algorithm produces more often, inflates it a second way. Removing same-artist repeats alone brings the naive value to 1.480 [1.432, 1.530]; a provenance-balanced re-training then yields a leakage-reduced, controlled Index of 1.15 [1.12, 1.19] on one frozen seed; 500 deterministic retrains of the same pipeline centre on 1.200 [1.156, 1.244], so the frozen value is a low draw (4th percentile) and the arm is reported as a seed distribution. The controlled Index is defined identically to the raw one, divided by its own partition’s geometry-free floor. There is no separate floor comparison to quote here either, since it would restate the same ratio a third time. Retraining at seven algorithmic-share levels traces a monotone dose-response curve, controlled, from 0.99 (the lowest contamination level, indistinguishable from parity on two of three seeds) to 1.46 (algorithm-only); the control shifts every level down by roughly 0.10–0.13 and leaves the slope intact.

The transferable result, stated generally. Nothing in the mechanism is about music. Let a log be a sequence of interaction events whose adjacency is produced by a mixture of two processes, one the user’s and one the system’s, with system share f, each event carrying an observed label naming the process that produced it; let E be a representation fit to co-occurrence in that sequence, and S a statistic that compares the two labelled classes through E’s geometry. Then S is biased in favour of the majority process, because E’s geometry is fit to that process’s adjacency, and the bias grows with f. Three conditions are needed: the two processes must differ in adjacency structure, E must be fit on the same stream S is computed over, and S must condition on the class label. The labels themselves never enter the fit, so the usual check for label leakage does not catch it, and setting f = 1/2 does not remove it, because balancing event counts does not balance the training signal each class contributes to a co-occurrence objective. Two families of S are demonstrated and no others: a partition-crossing rate ratio (the Bridge Index) and a geometry-only decodability score.

The planted null. A planted-null rerun of the leakage simulator, with the true Index set to 1.000 by construction, shows the stream-trained instrument reporting 1.107 [1.074, 1.140] at an algorithmic share of 0.80, so the channel can produce an apparent Index above 1 with no true asymmetry present in the quantity the Index measures. The excess grows with the algorithmic share of the training corpus, from 1.045 [1.012, 1.077] at f = 0.20; at the top level all ten simulated worlds are inflated and the paired excess is +0.114 [+0.084, +0.145]. The bias enters through the listener’s arm: at f = 0.80 the algorithmic crossing rate is unmoved (0.200 planted against 0.201 retrained) while the deliberate rate rises from 0.199 to 0.222. Rebalancing the training corpus to 50/50 does not repair it: the balanced arm sits above its planted truth at every level and is worst where the algorithm is the minority channel (1.257 against a planted 1.006 at f = 0.20). Four limits travel with that result. The world is synthetic, small and cleaner than the log, so those magnitudes are about the mechanism and not about this corpus’s numbers. The only estimator that passes the simulation’s own sanity gates is the same-artist-included one, because the generator lets a deliberate step repeat an artist while its k-NN algorithmic branches structurally cannot, which is the reverse of the log, where autoadvance is the channel that repeats artists. The bias runs upward at every level tested, so the simulation gives no support to the mirror-image account of the low end of the dose curve. And none of this reaches the values reported primarily here, which are computed on partitions built from external tags that the stream never trained: the result is evidence against the stream-trained partitions, the 1.61 this study started from and the 1.36–1.48 family around it, and it is the reason those are kept out of the headline (ledger 244).

The dose curve under a fixed community count. A reader can object that what moves along the dose axis is the partition’s granularity rather than the corpus’s provenance: in the published run the number of detected communities rises with algorithmic share, from a mean of 11.0 at f = 0.12 to 14.0 at f = 1.00. Holding the community count at K = 12, the median of the twenty-one counts the published run had already reported, by searching the Louvain resolution on the same k-NN graph at the same seed, and scoring every level only on the artist domain common to all seven levels, leaves the curve where it was. The level means run 0.982, 1.072, 1.188, 1.259, 1.367, 1.431 and 1.468, increasing at every step, with top and bottom three-seed ranges that do not overlap ([1.452, 1.480] against [0.976, 0.986]). The matched baseline computed in the same script runs 0.980 to 1.444, so the controlled endpoint span (0.486) and the uncontrolled one (0.464) agree to within the seed range at either endpoint, and the difference between them reads as noise rather than as a direction. Community count and vocabulary domain are therefore not what produces this curve (ledger 246).

What the dose axis holds fixed, and what it does not. Fixing the transition count at 18,170 does not fix the number of training pairs the objective consumes: the expected skip-gram pair count grows 1.7× from f = 0.12 to f = 1.00 (2.2× on the maximal-window count). A second pre-declared arm ran the axis the other way, holding the expected pair exposure at the lowest level’s value by shrinking each level’s transition budget to about 10,560 at f = 1.00, with K = 12 and common support kept. The level means still rise at every step, 0.983 to 1.357, with an endpoint span of 0.374 against 0.486 at fixed transition count: about three quarters of the slope survives the matching and about a quarter is inseparable from pair exposure or from the smaller corpora the matching requires. The match is made before frequent-token subsampling, after which the top level still carries about 9% more pairs than the bottom, and 20 of the 21 resolution searches hit K = 12. Below f = 0.5 part of the corpus is deliberate runs resampled with replacement (5,330 duplicated runs at the lowest level against none from f = 0.5 up); that duplication is the alternative this study does not remove.

What the corrected value is sensitive to. Perturbing the correction procedure itself (run-level downsampling, transition-level downsampling, inverse-propensity reweighting) then showed the corrected point estimate moves more under estimator choice than under the same-artist control itself: controlled variants span 1.026–1.345, one of which has a confidence interval including 1. The previously reported band, ≈1.15–1.30, is withdrawn: on the eight label-independent partitions the controlled Index instead ranges 1.03–1.14, a partition-sensitivity range, not an interval estimate. The headline is the leakage-free pure-Last.fm genre-family partition (1.05 [1.03, 1.08]); the higher-coverage genre-family partition reads 1.10 (session-block CI [1.08, 1.13]), with 39% of its labels propagated through the stream-trained embedding. Folding shuffle plays (20.5% of autoadvance landings) into the algorithmic arm gives 1.028 [1.003, 1.053] embedding-free and 1.069 [1.045, 1.093] on the higher-coverage variant. That is the lower bound on the comparator axis, and not the study’s lower bound: the lowest cells the paper prints are the singleton imputation of the untagged tail, 0.999 [0.983, 1.015], and the most inclusive definitional corner, bundled taps against a shuffle-included comparator, 1.012 [0.991, 1.033]. Strict autoadvance stays the primary comparator because a shuffled container is listener-authored. A same-album control beyond the same-artist one moves either value by at most 0.001.

Corroboration that isn’t. A device-verified slice points the same way: within the 11% of the corpus that watch sensors verify as headphone listening, the Index direction holds (1.20, same-artist included, session-block CI [1.08, 1.34]) on the same naive partition whose full-corpus value is 1.61, a subset consistency check, not an independent reproduction of the controlled estimate. Two provenance-free audio substrates are directionally consistent, not corroborating: CLAP embeddings give 1.047 (CI [1.026, 1.068]) and MERT 1.026 (CI [1.005, 1.046]) on the exact higher-coverage-recipe partition, with a coverage control confirming the missing previews don’t drive the agreement.

A portable contamination detector. The leakage catch also yields a portable detector: a provenance decodability probe (a linear probe reading tap-vs-autoplay off geometry-only features of an embedding’s transitions, scored above a refit label-shuffle floor) tracks training-corpus algorithmic share closely (Spearman 0.98 across 21 retrained embeddings), separates the contaminated full-stream embedding (0.18) from clean spaces (0.05–0.06), and stays correlated with contamination after controlling for the Index itself (partial r = 0.70, CI [0.39, 0.87]), a contamination score that is not a restatement of the Bridge Index.

The nulls that ran against it. A sequence-level surrogate-null re-audit of this same controlled Index (2026-09-04) found it survives the pooled intent-blind null on a fixed partition. A geometry-adaptive null, which lets the partition be re-learned, then killed the balanced-embedding arm (2026-09-20): over 500 deterministic surrogate retrains the null band centres on 1.213 and the observed 1.152 sits at its 1st percentile; retraining the real corpus gives 1.200 [1.156, 1.244], so the published 1.152 was one low draw of an unstable learned geometry, and the real retrains do not exceed their surrogate twins (35% of pairs). That arm is now a sensitivity row reported as a seed distribution. The embedding-free headline has no learned geometry and is unaffected. The higher-coverage value depends on labels assigned to 39% of artists by a nearest-neighbour rule; re-running that rule across 200 embedding seeds (post hoc) moves it by about 0.005 (1.1026, 5th to 95th percentile [1.0946, 1.1106]), which is small next to its own sampling interval, but the original embedding file was overwritten, so that row can be reproduced from the saved labels and not rebuilt from source. It shares the stream, the tagging scheme and 61% of its labels with the headline and does not corroborate it independently. Thirteen pre-declared gates ran against the headline itself. Three fired as kills, one substituted follow-up ran and passed, four proved unreachable by construction, one was informative and survived, and four added in revision did not fire, two of them on criteria we now judge had little power to fire: see the graveyard.

What the direction is stable to

  • Partition, seed, k, resolution. Eight label-independent partitions span 1.03–1.14.
  • Era. 1.061 [1.022, 1.100] before the 2023-09 logging changepoint, 1.053 [1.018, 1.088] after it.
  • Playback device. 1.084 [1.046, 1.124] within iOS, 1.043 [1.008, 1.077] within macOS.
  • Resampling unit. Clustering the bootstrap on departure artist (1,153 clusters) instead of session gives [1.021, 1.090], about 30% wider and still excluding 1. Against a naive transition-level resample the design effect of session clustering is 1.571, so the session-block interval is the conservative choice.
  • Standardisation. Hour-standardised 1.052 [1.028, 1.079]; platform-standardised 1.069 [1.042, 1.096] when both arms are standardised to the deliberate margin. The direction of that shift is a property of which margin is the target, not evidence about confounding.
  • Label permutation. A conditional label-permutation test (not randomization inference, since the deliberate-versus-served split is observational throughout) puts the null at 1.0022 [0.9858, 1.0187], with the observed 1.0532 above every one of 20,000 replicates. Only 1,550 of 3,271 session blocks carry both arms, so it bounds the headline’s within-session component rather than the headline.
  • Joint composition balancing. Balancing session position, hour and platform jointly lands between 1.053 and 1.074 [1.046, 1.102] depending on which arm is taken as the target population, with the direction and the exclusion of 1 unchanged either way.
  • Leave-one-out. All twenty-one year and family folds keep the estimate above 1.00 (range 1.021–1.100). The rule that did not fire required a fold to move the estimate by −0.053, about 1.7× the largest shift observed, with no reachability precheck run for it.
  • Power. This design could detect a value as small as 1.033 on the side away from 1. The observed 1.053 clears that floor, but this is the published interval restated, not independent corroboration.

What the direction is not stable to

  • The untagged tail. 33.8% of deliberate and 40.9% of autoplay different-artist transitions carry no external tag. Pooling untagged artists into one community gives 1.135 [1.109, 1.161]; giving each its own gives 0.999 [0.983, 1.015], the lowest cell the paper prints.
  • The two arm definitions at their most inclusive. The full 2×2 on the embedding-free partition: strict taps against strict autoadvance 1.053 [1.027, 1.080]; strict taps against a shuffle-included comparator 1.028 [1.003, 1.053]; bundled taps against strict autoadvance 1.037 [1.014, 1.060]; bundled taps against a shuffle-included comparator 1.012 [0.991, 1.033], an interval that brackets 1. The four cells are nested subsets of one transition stream, not four tests (post hoc, ledger 234).
  • Coverage matching. Restricting both arms to endpoints above a play-count threshold closes the between-arm coverage gap from 7.1 points to 0.2 and attenuates the Index, to 1.031 [1.004, 1.059] at 20 plays and 1.008 [0.977, 1.038] at 50. Interval support for a direction above 1 is confined to the weakly matched, long-tail-inclusive regime.
  • Session position. The k ≥ 5 bin holds 72% of taps and carries the result; the two thinnest of the four bins bracket 1 on 290 and 231 taps and settle nothing either way.
  • Hour of day. The six-hour bins do not separate from one another, but the contrast is underpowered: the day-minus-evening difference is +0.075 [−0.012, +0.161] against a design that detects a bin difference only at ≥0.12, and the evening bin’s own interval brackets 1, so it is uninformative rather than concordant.
  • Self-introduced destinations. 1.016 [0.971, 1.065], not distinguishable from parity.
  • Interval coverage at the higher-coverage value. On synthetic listeners the estimator is unbiased at the null and calibrated near the headline, but nominal 95% intervals cover about 86% at a planted 1.10 and 56% at a planted 1.20. Every quotation of 1.10 [1.08, 1.13] carries that flag.
  • The tail, attacked a second time. Audio-embedding imputation of the untagged tail reached only 70 of the 4,244 untagged artists, and its full-population bound returns the same value under random labels. It is reported as uninformative, not as a rescue.

The genre deconfounding, placebo-checked

An inverse-propensity re-derivation of the genre exposure bias barely moves the poles (Spearman 0.93 against the stratified ranking), but that agreement is weaker evidence than it looks: the weights move mass only mildly (Kish ESS 0.65), and content-free size-matched placebo partitions reproduce the agreement (median Spearman 0.89; 63% of 150 draws clear 0.85). What the placebo control does certify is the poles themselves: both anchor families are more extreme than every one of 150 size-matched placebo draws under both weightings (exploratory, post-hoc; one-sided Monte-Carlo p ≤ 0.0066 each). The claim is agreement plus placebo-extremity, not causality.

Same distance, different boundary

The natural gloss (the algorithm follows a similarity gradient, the listener jumps over it) predicts deliberate transitions should be longer in content space. They are not: the acoustic jump ratio of deliberate to algorithmic transitions is 1.01 on CLAP embeddings (CI [0.99, 1.04]) and 0.99 on MERT (CI [0.97, 1.01]) at the artist-level primary, though at track resolution a small distance effect is significant on both substrates (1.026–1.033), consistent with the community-boundary channel dominating but not exhausting the signal. Communities are not metric balls; a partition, not a metric, is the right instrument.

The design-fact reading

A skeptical reading holds that sequential recommenders are engineered to stay local (coherent, low-surprise continuation is the design objective), so the algorithm’s lane-keeping is a product fact, not a discovery about listeners. The central claim does not depend on adjudicating this: whether the gap reflects listener agency, recommender design, or both, the two channels demonstrably differ, and a log that mixes them misrepresents each. What the audit adds is that on the embedding-free partitions the difference holds under the measurement-artifact tests that could be run (embedding leakage, acoustic step size, first-order sequence structure), with label accuracy bounded only as far as the two tag-error runs above state, so there the channel difference reads as a property of the data rather than of the instruments. The psychological reading (why the human channel bridges) is left explicitly open; this log does not contain the experimental or context-conditioned data it would require.

◢◤ The graveyard

Eighteen findings explicitly killed, retracted, or withdrawn over the course of the study (sixteen have their own card in the public dashboard’s graveyard), each mapped to the check that killed it.

  1. Reverse music→physiology transfer: killed at the measured-target precheck: 80% of the target variable was forward-filled; deconfounded gain −0.77pp.
  2. Hawkes self-excitation of artist binges: killed by a surrogate null: apparent excess +0.48 collapses to ≈0 against an envelope-preserving surrogate.
  3. Listening→daytime stress, read as causal: killed by a surrogate null: onset event-study d=−0.06, p=0.25 (the descriptive ρ=−0.10 correlation stands on its own terms).
  4. Persistent-homology “taste loop”: killed by a session-structure surrogate: the observed H₁ lifetime sits below the null mean in every cell tested.
  5. The corrected-embedding leakage-correction point estimate: killed by perturbing the correction procedure itself: same-artist-controlled variants span 1.026–1.345 (one seed’s CI includes 1), the estimator choice moves the corrected value more than the same-artist control does; only the partition-sensitivity range (1.03–1.14) and the direction survive.
  6. Bridge Index topology (“agency is topological” framing) and its H₁ persistent-homology loop: killed by a first-order Markov corpus surrogate: the naive Bridge Index observed (1.613) falls below the surrogate’s 95th percentile (1.774, mean 1.731); the H₁ loop statistic likewise sits below its own Markov-null 95th percentile in both point-set variants; session first-order locality, not emergent topology, explains the signal.
  7. Tap-only Markov floor for the Bridge Index (a sequencing-agency reading): killed: a first-order Markov floor fit to the tap arm alone (0.5785 [0.5722, 0.5846]) exceeds the observed tap crossing rate (0.4665 [0.4524, 0.4813], p=1.0 against the floor): the listener’s own one-step habit predicts the crossing rate at least as well as any claim of deliberate sequencing does; a follow-up α-sweep on the same-artist-controlled stream holds the kill at every smoothing level on the balanced arm and at α≥1 on the higher-coverage partition (α=0.5 is an effective tie). “Deliberate” here names tap provenance, not agency.
  8. Multi-year “rising deliberate choice” trend: retracted: a 2023 logging-code addition (backbtn) plus an iOS device-mix shift account for the entire apparent rise.
  9. In-sample next-track predictability gap: retracted: full-vocabulary Cohen’s d falls from 0.50 in-sample to −0.02 under temporal held-out evaluation, a null.
  10. PAD contamination diagnostic: killed by its own audit: algebraically a restatement of the two cross-rates whose ratio is the Bridge Index it was meant to guard.
  11. Acoustic energy/brightness predicting heart-rate change: does not survive once selection and regression-to-mean are controlled.
  12. “Effort” (skipping through tracks) predicting future replay: does not survive once skipping is recognized as itself active listening.
  13. Comfort-replays tracking daily HRV: does not survive once a seven-year secular accumulation of old favorites is removed.
  14. Rolling “surprising-residual rate” over time: withdrawn: a vocabulary-freeze control shows roughly 85% of it is catalog turnover, not a behavioral signal.

◢◤ Implications for training on behavioral logs

A cross-lagged panel at the artist–month level asks who leads in the listener–recommender feedback loop. The raw monthly pair mixes a mechanical same-rotation carryover (autoplay keeps serving whatever a tap just put in rotation, 48% of all algorithmic serves fall inside that window) with the slower adaptive channel of actual interest. Isolating the adaptive channel by excluding same-session and 7-day mechanical carryover sharpens the forward path: a surge in deliberate taps of an artist predicts the algorithm serving that artist more the following month at β=0.29, against a reverse path of β≈0.05. At the resolution of which artists get amplified, this listener trains the recommender more than the recommender trains the listener, in the Granger sense that carryover-cleaned taps predict next-month serving several times more strongly than the reverse, not that the listener mechanically instructs the algorithm.

A companion analysis of autoplay streaks refines what a passive play is worth as training signal. Its own depth story died in a later audit. Descriptively, ratification (a strict deliberate tap of the same track within 30 days) runs 0.55→0.91 with streak depth over already-ratified material and 23.8%→1.5% over never-tapped material. A pre-declared within-track re-analysis (track fixed effects; track-matched conditional logit) shows those curves are composition: holding the track fixed, the familiar-material rise vanishes (FE slope −0.00008, CI straddling zero) and the never-tapped collapse keeps only ~8% of its pooled magnitude. Deep streaks reach less-ratifiable tracks; they barely change a given track’s odds. What stands are the levels: never-tapped autoplay earns a later deliberate tap 7.2% of the time against 28.7% for self-found music. The training implication is level-based, not depth-based: weight autoplay by prior deliberate contact with the track, not by streak depth.

The broader point generalizes past this one dataset. Any preference corpus harvested from a recommender-mediated interface (including the interaction logs increasingly used to train and evaluate generative models) inherits the same confound a raw streaming log does: deployed outputs shape which interactions get logged, and that logged data retrains the model. The provenance audit is the individual-level, directly-measured counterpart to that feedback loop, which population-scale recommender literature otherwise treats theoretically. The frame this paper argues for is a shift from treating logs as preferences to treating them as jointly authored, with the authorship measurable rather than assumed.

◢◤ Limitations

Single subject (n=1)
All quantitative results are from a single subject. Population claims are not licensed; the dataset is treated as a substrate for methodological development, not as evidence for population-level effects.
Life-event confounding
The seven-year window spans major life events (career, health, relocation) that plausibly co-drive mood, routine, device mix, and listening behavior. Detected change-points and era boundaries are descriptive segmentations of a lived history, not evidence of stable traits: the genre-provenance reversal, for example, cannot be distinguished on these data from a period effect in which context shifted both what was chosen and what was served.
Observer effect and instrument non-stationarity
Data collection concluded before any analysis began, so the analytic window contains no behavior the study could have altered. Reactivity is ruled out by construction, not merely tested for. The deeper issue this surfaced independently is that reason_start itself is not stationary: Spotify added a new logging code in 2023 and began logging short skips it had previously dropped, so longitudinal comparisons of provenance shares across client-version eras are unsafe. Cross-sectional results (the provenance decomposition and the genre-exposure bias) pool across the whole window and are not threatened by this; only over-time claims were, and those have been withdrawn.
Sparse audio features (now addressed)
The signal-level audio→physiology channel has now been run under pre-declared gates and is a null: an 18-dimensional librosa feature block adds ΔR² = 0.007 in-sample over circadian and activity controls but fails the track-identity surrogate (p = 0.07) and generalizes to nothing out-of-sample. The causal-null consolidation includes dense signal-level features.
Randomization-inference scoping
The randomization-inference license for single-case designs applies where a quasi-random instrument exists: here, the shuffle-channel tests. The deliberate-versus-served split itself is not randomized; it is the behavior under study, and is treated throughout as observational stratification, not as an experiment. General methodological claims (that intent stratification recovers preference structure obscured by platform-initiated exposure) are separated from idiographic empirical ones (the specific coefficients and effect sizes), which are deliberately not generalized beyond this subject.
