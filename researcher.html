Streaming Logs Are Not Preference Logs: A Single-Subject Provenance Audit of Who Explores in the Human–Recommender Loop

astralcrest · Independent Researcher · astralcrestlab@proton.me

Abstract

A streamed “play” is jointly produced by a listener and a recommender, yet streaming logs are routinely read as records of human preference. Spotify’s export records each play’s provenance (reason_start): a direct, per-play signal of listener intent among played items — not the item-exposure propensity the debiasing literature must estimate, which concerns unshown items no log sees; we do not recover it. We audit a 7-year, 97,427-play single-subject log (only 19.1% explicitly user-initiated), asking of every summary: does it survive conditioning on who initiated the play? Artist rankings and circadian profiles survive; genre composition does not; an in-sample predictability gap and a music–physiology coupling are retracted.

First, a finding: deliberate transitions cross communities more often than algorithmic ones (47% vs 37% on a provenance-balanced partition; calibrated permutation p=5×10⁻⁴, the resolution floor at B=2,000). The asymmetry is first-order, at community boundaries — the level a recommender’s next-step policy controls — not deep taste topology. We report its size as a band, Bridge Index ≈1.2–1.3, anchored by the leakage-checked genre-family partition (1.20: no embedding, no correction), corroborated by the CLAP audio partition (1.25, marginal over its floor).

Second, a catch: the first estimate was 1.61, inflated by an embedding trained on the algorithm-heavy stream. A balanced re-training removed roughly half the excess, the bias is a monotone dose-response in training-corpus algorithmic share, and the correction procedure itself gate-tested as estimator-dependent (1.14–1.47) — hence the band. Every claim ran an ordered audit, kill conditions fixed in code before each result was seen; on our eight new positive findings the gates killed five, caveated three, passed none unqualified. The bridge direction survived every gate — what died were point estimates. The audit and Index run on any provenance-labeled log; cross-listener validation is the one untested step.

Positioning vs. related work

The algorithmic-confounding lineage (Chaney et al.) establishes that user-behavior claims are invalid without accounting for exposure — but demonstrates it on simulated or rating-matrix data with an inferred or latent exposure variable; propensity-scoring methods likewise estimate a click propensity from the interaction matrix itself. This setting differs in what is observed: Spotify’s reason_start field records the initiation channel of each play directly. That is not the exposure variable the debiasing lineage estimates — their propensities concern which items were surfaced, including unshown items no listening log can see; this paper does not recover that counterfactual. What direct observation buys is narrower: for summaries of played items, deconfounding reduces to conditioning on an observed intent label rather than positing a latent exposure. Villermet et al. disentangle organic, algorithmic, and editorial curation across roughly nine thousand Deezer users and find the balance strongly user-dependent (“filter niches,” not a uniform bubble); Shakespeare et al. show that whether algorithmic curation reads as diversity-reducing depends on the scale of aggregation, so genre-level conclusions are inherently fragile to representation choices. This paper is the single-subject, epistemic-audit counterpart to that population work: rather than characterize the curation mix, it asks which downstream summaries survive conditioning on a directly-observed intent signal, and the genre-level fragility result reproduces the scale- and heterogeneity-driven findings of that literature at n=1.

Method in one screen

Provenance classes

Every play is stratified on reason_start into three tiers: direct tap (19.1% — clickrow/playbtn/backbtn/fwdbtn/remote; a strict deliberate-selection subset, clickrow/playbtn/remote, is 11.5%), autoadvance (80.2% — trackdone, the platform selecting the next queue item), and ambiguous (0.5% — appload/null/unknown, excluded from intent-stratified analyses). Independently, 16.7% of plays carry shuffle=True. Autoadvance and shuffle-driven plays together put the algorithmic-exposure share at roughly 95% of the corpus.

The gate sequence

Every candidate claim runs an ordered sequence of pass/fail gates, kill condition fixed in code before the result is seen:

0pre-declared kill condition → 0bmeasured-target precheck → 1chronological held-out eval → 2perturbation robustness → 3power analysis (nulls) → 4surrogate / permutation null → 5E-value confounder bound

killed at the first failed gate · caveated if the core gates pass but a bounding gate flags a limit · survived only if every applicable gate passes — skipped gates are not passes.

The Bridge Index and the leakage correction

Splitting the log by provenance yields two transition graphs over the same 804 artists: the listener’s deliberate taps and the platform’s autoadvance plays. On communities detected from an intent-independent artist-embedding k-NN graph (Louvain, k=10, resolution 1.0), deliberate transitions cross community boundaries 47.4% of the time against autoadvance’s 36.7% (balanced-partition rates). The Bridge Index is the ratio of the listener’s cross-community rate to the algorithm’s.

The naive full-stream embedding gives 1.61 — inflated because the embedding is trained on a stream that is 6.4× algorithm-dominated, so algorithm-adjacency shapes the communities themselves and deflates the algorithm’s measured cross-rate. A provenance-balanced re-training yields BI = 1.29, removing roughly half the excess over 1. Retraining at seven algorithmic-share levels traces a monotone dose-response curve from 1.09 (tap-heavy) to 1.58 (algorithm-only). Perturbing the correction procedure itself — run-level downsampling, transition-level downsampling, inverse-propensity reweighting — then showed the corrected point estimate is itself estimator-dependent (1.14–1.47). The honest report is therefore a band, ≈1.2–1.3, anchored by two partitions that need no embedding and no correction at all: the external genre-family partition (1.20) and the CLAP audio partition (1.25, marginal over its own floor) — partitions sharing no substrate with the log or with each other (pairwise ARI 0.03–0.08).

The design-fact reading

A skeptical reading holds that sequential recommenders are engineered to stay local — coherent, low-surprise continuation is the design objective — so the algorithm’s lane-keeping is a product fact, not a discovery about listeners. The central claim does not depend on adjudicating this: whether the gap reflects listener agency, recommender design, or both, the two channels demonstrably differ, and a log that mixes them misrepresents each. What the audit adds is that the difference survives every measurement-artifact attack mounted against it (embedding leakage, acoustic step size, first-order sequence structure), so the channel difference is a property of the data rather than of the instruments. The psychological reading — why the human channel bridges — is left explicitly open; this log does not contain the experimental or context-conditioned data it would require.

The graveyard

Fourteen findings explicitly killed, retracted, or withdrawn over the course of the study, each mapped to the check that killed it.

  1. Reverse music→physiology transfer — killed at the measured-target precheck: 80% of the target variable was forward-filled; deconfounded gain −0.77pp.
  2. Hawkes self-excitation of artist binges — killed by a surrogate null: apparent excess +0.48 collapses to ≈0 against an envelope-preserving surrogate.
  3. Listening→daytime stress, read as causal — killed by a surrogate null: onset event-study d=−0.06, p=0.25 (the descriptive ρ=−0.10 correlation stands on its own terms).
  4. Persistent-homology “taste loop” — killed by a session-structure surrogate: the observed H₁ lifetime sits below the null mean in every cell tested.
  5. The 1.29 leakage-correction point estimate — killed by perturbing the correction procedure itself: variants span 1.14–1.47, so only the band survives.
  6. Multi-year “rising deliberate choice” trend — retracted: a 2023 logging-code addition (backbtn) plus an iOS device-mix shift account for the entire apparent rise.
  7. In-sample next-track predictability gap — retracted: full-vocabulary Cohen’s d falls from 0.50 in-sample to −0.02 under temporal held-out evaluation, a null.
  8. PAD contamination diagnostic — killed by its own audit: algebraically a restatement of the two cross-rates whose ratio is the Bridge Index it was meant to guard.
  9. Acoustic energy/brightness predicting heart-rate change — does not survive once selection and regression-to-mean are controlled.
  10. “Effort” (skipping through tracks) predicting future replay — does not survive once skipping is recognized as itself active listening.
  11. Comfort-replays tracking daily HRV — does not survive once a seven-year secular accumulation of old favorites is removed.
  12. Rolling “surprising-residual rate” over time — withdrawn: a vocabulary-freeze control shows roughly 85% of it is catalog turnover, not a behavioral signal.
  13. Cross-log portability of the intent proxy (Last.fm-1K demo) — killed by its own pre-declared directional gate: the inferred-class diversity gap came out coherently wrong-signed (median −0.34, 99% of per-user CIs excluding zero on the negative side), and a seeded control shows the same readout inverts on the training log itself — valid intervals, invalid construct.
  14. The depth-response of passivity decay (delegation rises / never-tapped collapses with streak depth) — killed by holding the track fixed: track fixed-effects and a conditional logit show the familiar-material rise vanishes and the never-tapped collapse keeps ~8% of its slope — composition, not depth. The 7.2% vs 28.7% levels survive.

Implications for training on behavioral logs

A cross-lagged panel at the artist–month level asks who leads in the listener–recommender feedback loop. The raw monthly pair mixes a mechanical same-rotation carryover (autoplay keeps serving whatever a tap just put in rotation — 48% of all algorithmic serves fall inside that window) with the slower adaptive channel of actual interest. Isolating the adaptive channel by excluding same-session and 7-day mechanical carryover sharpens the forward path: a surge in deliberate taps of an artist predicts the algorithm serving that artist more the following month at β=0.29, against a reverse path of β≈0.05. At the resolution of which artists get amplified, this listener trains the recommender more than the recommender trains the listener, in the Granger sense that carryover-cleaned taps predict next-month serving several times more strongly than the reverse — not that the listener mechanically instructs the algorithm.

A companion analysis of autoplay streaks refines what a passive play is worth as training signal — and its own depth story died in a later audit. Descriptively, ratification (a strict deliberate tap of the same track within 30 days) runs 0.55→0.91 with streak depth over already-ratified material and 23.8%→1.5% over never-tapped material. A pre-declared within-track re-analysis (track fixed effects; track-matched conditional logit) shows those curves are composition: holding the track fixed, the familiar-material rise vanishes (FE slope −0.00008, CI straddling zero) and the never-tapped collapse keeps only ~8% of its pooled magnitude. Deep streaks reach less-ratifiable tracks; they barely change a given track’s odds. What stands are the levels: never-tapped autoplay earns a later deliberate tap 7.2% of the time against 28.7% for self-found music. The training implication is level-based, not depth-based: weight autoplay by prior deliberate contact with the track, not by streak depth.

The broader point generalizes past this one dataset. Any preference corpus harvested from a recommender-mediated interface — including the interaction logs increasingly used to train and evaluate generative models — inherits the same confound a raw streaming log does: deployed outputs shape which interactions get logged, and that logged data retrains the model. The provenance audit is the individual-level, directly-measured counterpart to that feedback loop, which population-scale recommender literature otherwise treats theoretically. The frame this paper argues for is a shift from treating logs as preferences to treating them as jointly authored, with the authorship measurable rather than assumed.

Limitations

Single subject (n=1)
All quantitative results are from a single subject. Population claims are not licensed; the dataset is treated as a substrate for methodological development, not as evidence for population-level effects.
Life-event confounding
The seven-year window spans major life events (career, health, relocation) that plausibly co-drive mood, routine, device mix, and listening behavior. Detected change-points and era boundaries are descriptive segmentations of a lived history, not evidence of stable traits — the genre-provenance reversal, for example, cannot be distinguished on these data from a period effect in which context shifted both what was chosen and what was served.
Observer effect and instrument non-stationarity
Data collection concluded before any analysis began, so the analytic window contains no behavior the study could have altered — reactivity is ruled out by construction, not merely tested for. The deeper issue this surfaced independently is that reason_start itself is not stationary: Spotify added a new logging code in 2023 and began logging short skips it had previously dropped, so longitudinal comparisons of provenance shares across client-version eras are unsafe. Cross-sectional results (the provenance decomposition and the genre-exposure bias) pool across the whole window and are not threatened by this; only over-time claims were, and those have been withdrawn.
Randomization-inference scoping
The randomization-inference license for single-case designs applies where a quasi-random instrument exists — here, the shuffle-channel tests. The deliberate-versus-served split itself is not randomized; it is the behavior under study, and is treated throughout as observational stratification, not as an experiment. General methodological claims (that intent stratification recovers preference structure obscured by platform-initiated exposure) are separated from idiographic empirical ones (the specific coefficients and effect sizes), which are deliberately not generalized beyond this subject.
