my soundtrack

twenty-three songs i wrote for this, keyed and tempo-matched so one hands into the next. the first press anywhere on the site starts them; the dock at the bottom holds play, next and mute, and your choice carries across every page.

every claim was pre-declared and refereed; the ones that died are still here, marked. nothing on this page says music changes the body, or that the app knows me. the strictest count, the genre numbers and the crossing rate are directions with intervals, not sizes. counts are plays of 30 seconds or more.

how deep

the count

19of 100 my hand, counting every press. 11.5 by the strictest count: shuffle 17, the queue 64. the river draws the first count; the strictest count has no month-by-month series, so the switch changes only the headline.

the river

height is how much i listened that month; the bands are whose hand started the plays. october 2023: the app changed how it records a start, so the bands before and after that line are not the same instrument.

each month's three counts come from the play log (plays of 30 seconds or more). a mote is a fixed number of plays; motes are placed at random inside their band and drift, so the picture is a count, not a position. nothing in the river is a test.

the clock

one fixed clock all year (no per-play timezone or daylight-saving data was kept), so the hours are only roughly local. a share, not a volume.

the shape

the map

artists that shared my evenings sit near each other. far apart means little. the map learned from every play, and the app started most of them. the map is the picture, not the proof.

a personal word2vec on 5,489 sittings (a 30-minute silence closes one), then umap to three dimensions, cosine, 18 neighbors. 1,986 artists placed; the rest wait at the edge. the glow around each star is the plays themselves, one mote per dozen, landing on the artist in proportion to the hand that started them.

the embedding is trained on all 97,427 plays, so its geometry reflects the queue as much as me. that is why no finding on this page is read off the map; the refereed numbers below come from genre tags, not from distances.

families

counted by play, ambient and lo-fi reach me mostly through the app; hip-hop and r&b are the ones i reach for.

arm rate = tapped share within a family over the tapped share overall, strict definition, same-artist control, 28-day block bootstrap. families are a collapse of 1,067 tags; the fine tags only feed the tag-noise check.

the crossing

a genre-family crossing rate per arm, same-artist pairs excluded, intervals from a 28-day block bootstrap. the earlier embedding-distance version of this number was withdrawn; the sky never claims it.

who introduced us

unit = an artist with two plays or more. introducer = the arm of our first play: my tap (track row, play button, remote, skip forward or back), shuffle, or the queue on its own. an artist is "still with me" while its last play is within 90 days of the end of the log. survival by kaplan-meier, interval from a 28-day block bootstrap over the months we met. pre-registered on a hash before the numbers. this is selection, not treatment: a first tap is usually a search for a name i already carried, so the sentence says what i already knew, never that the queue fails.

my hand, four more ways

the body

each lane was pre-declared on a hash before any number: unit, clock, arms, model, kill rule, and the smallest effect it could detect at 80 percent power (the blind spot). intervals come from a 28-day block bootstrap; nulls are tested against within-day permutations. a lane is "killed" when a control removes it, "reversed" when the sign came out against the pre-declared direction, "null" when the interval holds zero with the blind spot stated, "descriptive" when it is a number with caveats and no claim. nothing on this page is a claim that music changes the body.

the tiers

the graveyard

sixteen earlier claims, each with what killed it. a claim stays dead once a valid control removes it; it never comes back as a weaker version of itself.

the record

one groove per month, 2019 at the rim and 2026 at the center; the color of each mote is the hand that pressed play. the record you can enter is the same data as a sculpture, with six rooms and the album as the soundtrack.

97,427 plays of 30 seconds or more. the shares in every room are the ones on this page: 19 / 17 / 64, and 11.5 by the strictest count.

the ledger of every claim, alive and dead, lives in the lab; the methods and pre-registrations in the report. october 2023: the app changed how it records a start.
